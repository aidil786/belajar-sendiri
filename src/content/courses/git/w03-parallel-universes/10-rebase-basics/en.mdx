<Card id="hook" type="hook">
<Interactive name="TermReplay" scenario="w03-l10" commands={['git log --oneline --graph --all']} label="The log forks. main has Make heading teal. about, where HEAD is, has Add about page and Say what I like." />

main moved on while you built about. You want your work on top of the newest main. Without a merge commit.
</Card>

<Card id="see-rebase" type="see">
<Interactive name="GraphScene" scenario="w03-l10" commands={['git rebase main']} label="The two about commits lift off and land after Make heading teal. The history becomes one straight line." />

`git rebase main` replays your commits on top of main, one by one. Like moving your work to the end of the line. That's a **<Term id="rebase">rebase</Term>**.
</Card>

<Hint>Type `git rebase main`, then press Enter.</Hint>

<Card id="see-ids" type="see">
<Interactive name="TermReplay" scenario="w03-l10" commands={['git log --oneline -n 2', 'git rebase main', 'git log --oneline -n 2']} label="Before the rebase, the two about commits have one pair of short hashes. After it, the same messages have new hashes." />

Look at the ids. Same messages, new hashes! A rebase makes new copies of your commits.
</Card>

<Card id="win" type="win">
You can now rebase a branch onto the newest main.
</Card>

<Card id="deeper" type="deeper">
A rebase can stop on a conflict too. Fix the file and `git add` it. Then run `git rebase --continue`. Changed your mind? `git rebase --abort` puts everything back. The bottom marker names the commit being replayed.

</Card>
