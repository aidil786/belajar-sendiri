Two ways to bring about into main: merge or rebase. Your team argues about it. Who wins?
</Card>

<Card id="see-golden" type="see">
<Interactive name="GraphScene" scenario="w03-l10" commands={['git rebase main']} showLost label="After the rebase, new copies of the about commits sit after main. The old commits stay behind as dashed dots." />

Rebase makes new copies. The old commits live on with anyone who has them. Two versions! So never rebase commits others already have. That's the **<Term id="golden-rule">golden rule</Term>**.
</Card>

<Card id="see-cherry" type="see">
<Interactive name="GraphScene" scenario="w03-l12-pick" commands={['git cherry-pick experiments~1']} label="A copy of Fix README intro appears on main. The experiments branch keeps all three of its commits." />

Sometimes you need just one commit. `git cherry-pick` copies one commit onto your branch. One cherry, not the whole tree. That's a **<Term id="cherry-pick">cherry-pick</Term>**.
</Card>

<Card id="try-pick" type="try">

</Card>

<Card id="win" type="win">
You can now choose merge or rebase, and copy one commit.

<Card id="deeper" type="deeper">
Many teams rebase their own branch, then merge it. Both are fine with the golden rule. `git cherry-pick -x` adds a line saying where the copy came from. A clash? Fix the file, `git add`, then `git cherry-pick --continue`. Or `git cherry-pick --abort`.
</Card>
