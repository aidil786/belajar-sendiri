<Card id="see-replace" type="see">
<Interactive name="ManagersBoard" scenario="k2-restart" target="shop" actions={['deletePod']} goal={{ healed: 1 }} success="A new tray, with a new name, took its place." label="The board wants 3 and sees 3. Delete a tray: it sees 2 and makes a new pod with a new name." />

<Card id="see-rollout-restart" type="see">
<Interactive name="KubeReplay" scenario="k2-restart" commands={['kubectl get pods', 'kubectl rollout restart deployment/shop', 'kubectl rollout status deployment/shop --timeout=180s', 'kubectl get pods']} label="Before: 3 pods. `kubectl rollout restart` replaces every tray, a few at a time. Same recipe, fresh trays. Compare the names before and after.
</Card>

<Card id="check" type="check">

<Card id="win" type="win">
You can now tell a container restart from a pod replacement.
</Card>

<Card id="deeper" type="deeper">
Restarts wait longer each time: 10 s, 20 s, 40 s, up to 5 minutes. After 10 minutes without a crash, the wait resets. A new pod also gets a new IP address. That is why Services exist (World K3).

Real rep (optional): on a throwaway cluster, watch `kubectl get pods -w` during a rollout restart.
</Card>
