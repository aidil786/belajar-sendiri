<Card id="try-aina" type="try">
Give Aina her own user with a home: `sudo useradd -m -s /bin/bash aina`. Then add her to the kitchen team: `sudo usermod -aG kitchen aina`.

<Interactive name="LinuxSim" scenario="linux-l2-users" goal={{ userExists: ['aina'], inGroup: { aina: 'kitchen' } }} hints={['First: sudo useradd -m -s /bin/bash aina', 'Then: sudo usermod -aG kitchen aina', 'Check: groups aina']} solution={['sudo useradd -m -s /bin/bash aina', 'sudo usermod -aG kitchen aina']} success="Aina has her own key and joined the kitchen team." />

<Hint>`-m` makes her home folder. `-aG` adds a group.</Hint>
</Card>

<Card id="check-append" type="check">
Why `usermod -aG kitchen`, and not just `-G kitchen`?

<Interactive name="Choice" options={[{ label: '-G alone replaces her other teams. -a adds instead.', correct: true, why: 'Forget the -a and she quietly drops out of her other groups.' }, { label: 'They do the same thing', why: 'Not quite. Without -a, -G sets the full list of her extra groups.' }, { label: '-a means admin', why: '-a means append: add to the list.' }]} />
</Card>

<Card id="win" type="win">
You can now tell users from groups, and use sudo with care.
</Card>

<Card id="deeper" type="deeper">
`sudo -i` opens a root shell: the prompt ends in `#` and a root badge shows. Type `exit` to give the key back. Without sudo, `useradd` says `command not found`: admin tools live in `/usr/sbin`, outside your PATH.

Real rep (optional): on your own Linux, run `id` and `groups`. Then `sudo -l` shows what sudo lets you do.
</Card>
